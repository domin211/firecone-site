About Firecone

Firecone started years ago as a small Minecraft server run by friends. After hitting repeated problems with commercial hosting—lag spikes, unclear limits, and poor support—we decided to build our own stack. What began as tinkering and experimenting slowly grew into a full platform.

Over time we learned to manage dedicated hardware, containerize game servers, and automate backups. Friends of friends asked to join, then whole communities. By focusing on stability and transparency, the project evolved into a hosting provider that does things differently.

Today Firecone provides free and low-cost hosting to a wide range of players and communities. We keep things simple: publish the limits up front, explain how the system works, and share what we learn through documentation and support. The project first went live in 2019.

Mission & values

  • Transparency: Clear resource limits, status updates, and postmortems so users always know what’s happening.
  • Reliability: Practical infrastructure choices and backup policies designed to keep servers online and recoverable.
  • Community: Treat hosting as a shared effort—documentation, support, and tools written by people who actually run servers themselves.

Who we are

Firecone is run by a small team with backgrounds in software development and system administration. We’re not backed by venture funding or faceless corporations—we run lean, with a focus on practical engineering and community-first design.

Our admins and support staff also host their own servers, which means the people answering tickets actually know what it’s like to debug plugins, configure permissions, or fight with tick lag. Four admins share on-call duties.

Infrastructure

Firecone rents a handful of dedicated nodes from trusted European providers and keeps spare capacity reserved for scheduled events. Hardware specs are available by request for a given server, as we swap servers around nodes when required or beneficial.

Our footprint currently covers Helsinki, Falkenstein, and Montreal. We rely on the datacenters’ upstream transit rather than custom links. Each node runs containerized Minecraft instances with isolated file systems. Backups, plugins, and configuration files stay separated from neighboring servers, and maintenance windows are announced at least a week ahead with rollback plans if updates misbehave.

How we stay accountable

  • Publish uptime stats, incident notes, and roadmap updates on the status page.
  • Use Discord as the main feedback loop—feature requests and pain points get discussed openly.
  • Keep offsite backups so worlds survive even if hardware doesn’t.
  • Run regular maintenance windows with clear announcements and rollback plans.

Inclusion & accessibility

Firecone servers are open to a wide range of players and creators. We aim to keep our tools understandable, our documentation accessible, and our communities welcoming. Feedback on accessibility is reviewed alongside technical improvements so everyone can participate.
